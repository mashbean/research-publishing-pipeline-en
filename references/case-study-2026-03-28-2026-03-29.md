# Case Study: 2026-03-28 to 2026-03-29

## Scope

This case study summarizes the actual production flow and failure points for two research articles produced on 2026-03-28 and 2026-03-29:

1. An article about advocacy workers and shifts in funding priorities.
2. An article about IT procurement and digital procurement.

## Article One

### Title

Who Funds Public Action: How Funding Priorities Shape Advocacy Careers

### Outcome

- Published successfully.
- Canonical short URL: `/blog/2026/0328-3lr3dx/`

### Notes

- Organization-level evidence was stronger than individual career evidence.
- Individual career impact was mostly inferred through organizational adjustment.
- Blog writing style rules were applied.

## Article Two

### Original Title

Two Paths in Digital Procurement: Institutional Divergence from Market Concentration to Startup Growth

### Final Published Title

Does IT Procurement Create Monopolies or Reward Innovation?

### Outcome

- Canonical short URL: `/blog/2026/0328-bx0u50/`
- Revised and redeployed on 2026-03-29, then live verification passed.

## Process Problems Exposed

1. The writing pipeline and repository publishing pipeline were mixed together.
2. There was no translation layer between Deep Research output and publishable draft.
3. Self-citation and citation sanitation lacked a dedicated step.
4. A route fix accidentally linked the wrong short code for two articles from the same day.
5. Progress updates were not tied tightly enough to actual execution, causing pending narration.

## Rules That Became Permanent

- Deep Research must receive a packet and should not start from a blank slate.
- A research draft cannot be used directly as a publish draft.
- When asked about recent days, read the daily memory for the exact dates instead of relying only on semantic matches.
- Publishing is complete only after live URL verification passes, not after push.
