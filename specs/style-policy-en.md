# English Writing Style Policy

## Goal

Turn a research draft into a publishable long-form English blog article while avoiding report-like prose, executive-summary voice, and generic AI scaffolding.

## Mandatory Rules

1. Avoid repetitive `not X but Y` framing.
   - Do not use it as the default way to create contrast.
   - Rewrite the sentence around the actual claim.

2. Avoid colon overuse in body prose.
   - Headings, field names, and paths may use colons.
   - Body sentences should usually read as complete prose.

3. Avoid report-like scaffolding.
   - Do not make the article read like an executive summary.
   - Avoid repeated phrases such as `this article will`, `this paper argues`, and `the purpose of this research is`.

4. Avoid prompt leakage.
   - Do not include internal drafting language such as `the tone should be`, `make this more formal`, or `I need to maintain`.

5. Preserve analytical depth.
   - Long sentences are allowed when they earn their length.
   - Paragraphs may be layered.
   - Transitions should carry reasoning, not filler.

## Rewrite Direction

- Make the piece readable first, then preserve the research depth.
- State the argument before arranging citations around it.
- Use the opening to tell general readers what the article is trying to answer.
- Treat the research report and the blog article as separate writing modes.

## Final Pass

Search and rewrite:

- `not`
- `but`
- `this article will`
- `this paper`
- repeated colon-heavy sentences

The goal is not mechanical deletion. Rewrite sentence structure when a pattern weakens the prose.
